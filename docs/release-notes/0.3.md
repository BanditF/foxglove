# Foxglove 0.3

*Release notes for app 0.3.0, in progress: entries are added as PRs merge to main after the
0.2 notes (#740).*

## App

- **Screen glow (#496):** a new effect on the Scenes page, last in the list: your lights take the
  colours at the edges of your screens, like a TV's ambient light. Each device follows the
  nearest stretch of the nearest screen's edge on the Room map (drag it to change that), and
  black bars of letterboxed films are skipped. The first Start asks whether Foxglove may read
  your screen; only **Allow and start** says yes, and **Stop allowing** under the effect takes it
  back. Nothing else starts it without that yes (a profile, the last look after a restart, a
  wallpaper's look). While it reads, the tray icon shows an eye, the tooltip says "Screen glow
  on" and the tray panel has a Screen glow row with **Stop**. It pauses while a game is full
  screen (video keeps glowing), the PC is locked, the display is off, Foxglove is paused,
  another app has the lights or the screen can't be read. **Smoothing:** Quick, Even (the
  default) or Calm (the default when Windows' animation effects are off); a light never swings
  between dark and bright more than 3 times a second. At most 20 updates a second reach the
  lights. The picture never leaves Foxglove and is never saved. Off by default.
  When a full-screen game starts, the lights fade to their own colours over a second instead
  of jumping; when the screen can't be read they hold for 2 s, then fade over 3 s. When the
  game ends, Screen glow eases back in (#1085). HDR desktops are read too (#1085), where they
  used to end as "Can't read the screen right now". On a still screen Screen glow no longer
  wakes 30 times a second: it works only when the picture changes.

- **Hide an app from the knob (#952):** the knob's click skips apps whose program has no window
  at all (a background helper holding a sound stream; apps in the tray still count) and apps on
  a hidden list. The tray panel's new Knob row shows the app the knob is on with **Hide from
  knob**; one click moves the knob on and hides the app from then on, with Undo until the panel
  closes. Settings, Keyboard page, Knob card lists the hidden apps with **Show again**, and **Add
  an app…** hides one before the knob ever lands on it. Apps are matched by their program file,
  so a hide survives restarts and updates. Foxglove ships a short default list as data
  (`app/resources/knob-hidden.toml`); showing one of those again is kept over later lists.
  The tray's Knob row and each Settings row show the app's icon before its name (#981).
  An app on Foxglove's own list shows its icon even when it has no sound right now: the card
  looks once for the running program when it opens.
- **The knob skips apps that only listen (#1060) and keeps one row per app (#1061):** an app
  whose sound level only follows what the speakers already play (lighting or a wallpaper that
  reacts to music) no longer joins the knob's cycle; it joins if it starts playing sound of its
  own. Every sound stream of one app is one stop on the knob: a turn moves all of them by 2 %
  from their own levels, a double click mutes or unmutes all of them, the knob shows the
  loudest, and "n of m" counts apps. A program that plays sound for another app and has no
  window of its own (an app's built-in web view) belongs to the app that started it; a game
  started by a launcher stays its own app. Games whose program Windows won't name can now be hidden from the knob. Two apps with one
  name add their folder ("Vivaldi · Vivaldi Snapshot"). The Knob card's intro and the tray
  row's tooltip say so. Each knob click logs its app's processes and programs.
- **Master volume on a device that ignores Windows volume (#1064):** some headsets' USB dongles
  take Windows' volume into hardware and sometimes don't apply it, so the knob, the volume keys
  and Windows' slider moved the number but not the sound. For a device whose volume Windows hands
  to the hardware, Settings, Keyboard page, Knob card has a new MASTER VOLUME part with
  **Foxglove sets this device's volume** (on by default for devices Foxglove knows do this,
  first the Xbox-protocol headset dongle). While it is on,
  Foxglove holds the device one volume step below the top and turns every app down by master's
  amount (Windows' volume curve), new apps as they start, so the knob, Windows' slider and the
  volume keys all work, both ways. A change from Windows' slider or keys shows Foxglove's master
  card above Windows' flyout. Each app's own level is kept and put back exactly when it is turned
  off, the default device changes or Foxglove quits; a journal puts it back after a crash. Master
  mute mutes the apps; unmuting brings back only the ones it muted. A drag on Windows' slider is
  applied when you let go, and a drag down never makes anything louder. An app that keeps putting
  its own volume back (a game's slider) is left alone after about ten times in ten seconds; the
  tray Knob row's tooltip says so.
  The knob's app rows show each app's own level. The left screen's volume bar shows master.
  Keyboard health has a Knob · Master volume row.
  Follow-ups (#1070): the toggle's screen-reader description is the device's name and its second
  line. Turning it off or quitting while master is muted keeps the apps muted until the device is
  unmuted. Volume keys step both ways alike on devices that report coarse steps, a drag that
  starts with one step is a drag, and if Foxglove can't read the levels it saved, it keeps them and pauses master volume until it
  can (the Knob card says so). The knob's unmute also unmutes the apps a muted master left muted.
- **Keyboard health, live wallpaper art:** a piece with no name reads "a piece with no name".
- **Live Board after a connect (#1044):** the wallpaper's Live Board shows the layer and the
  right screen's art again after the keyboard connects or comes back from an unplug. The layer
  shows at once (the base layer until the keyboard reports another), and when the first art
  read through the right half times out, Foxglove reads it again by itself, a few times over
  about 20 s, instead of showing LAYER "--" and NO FRAMES for the rest of the session.
  A piece Foxglove sent to the right screen keeps its name and frames after Foxglove restarts
  or the keyboard is plugged in again: Foxglove remembers the last piece it sent. A sent piece
  Foxglove has no record of is called "A piece from this PC" on the wallpaper too (#1075).
- **Live wallpaper art in Keyboard health (#1053):** while the live feed is on, a new row warns
  when the right screen shows art but the wallpaper has none for it and shows its placeholder;
  the log gets one line when that starts.
- **Overlay and a display that keeps dropping (#1046):** when the overlay's display drops out
  and comes back again and again (a sleeping monitor's link, here every ~10 s), the overlay no
  longer jumps between the displays with each drop. From the second drop within a minute it
  stays on the main display, and it goes back once that display has stayed for a minute. A
  single unplug or power-off still moves it to the main display and back as before.
- **Liquid cooler on the Lights page (#614):** a device Foxglove can't control yet now says
  what it does instead: "Lights not controllable yet: it keeps its own effect (rainbow)." for
  the OMEN liquid cooler, from the catalog, and the same line without a name for a device whose
  effect isn't known. The cooler lists its pump ring and radiator fans as two zones. Foxglove
  sends nothing to it.
- **Pill rows from the keyboard and screen readers (#1085):** every row of pills that picks one
  (Effect, Profile, Lights Off/On, Smoothing, Palette, Background, Direction, Window theme, the
  calibrator's choices and others) is now a radio group: a screen reader names the row and says
  which pill is checked. Tab goes to the selected pill and the next Tab leaves the row; the arrow
  keys move and pick within the row, wrapping round; Home and End go to the first and last;
  Space or Enter picks. The pills look as before.

## Plugins

- **Tiles and pages for Hideout (#975):** Mic mute, the focus timer, OBS, Corsair iCUE and
  OpenRGB each ship a `module.toml` next to their `plugin.toml`, with a tray tile and a page for
  the Hideout hub (named Focus timer, OBS, Corsair iCUE and OpenRGB there). Foxglove still reads
  `plugin.toml` and nothing changes in its Plugins tab.
- **OpenRGB, port busy:** in Hideout, a busy port 5568 shows "Port 5568 is busy" with **Try
  again** instead of stopping the plugin (which became "Stopped after 5 crashes"). In Foxglove it
  stops as before.
- **App watch wording (#991):** Foxglove's install dialog and Plugins tab say "see when OBS
  Studio opens or closes" for an `apps.watch` permission: when the program runs, or is installed
  and Windows lists it under App Paths, its name comes from the program file (as Task Manager
  shows it); otherwise the plugin's name spells it, or the file's name without `.exe`.
- **Corsair iCUE, setup:** without the SDK file the tile says "Needs setup" with the steps on its
  page; in Hideout the SDK path is a file setting with **Choose file**, and a new file takes
  effect at once, with no turning the plugin off and on (Foxglove too).
- **Hideout app watch (#991):** the hub notices when the apps its modules name (Corsair iCUE,
  OBS, OpenRGB, ...) start and quit, without checking on a timer. Their tiles show once the app
  has run on this PC, and Corsair iCUE now tells "iCUE said no" (iCUE runs with SDK access off:
  **How to**, with that step first) from "iCUE isn't open"; for its first 10 s after iCUE
  starts the tile still says "iCUE isn't open", while iCUE connects. The permission line will read
  "see whether iCUE is running". Modules' images (`ui.asset.put`) reach their pages.
- **Hideout hub, modules live (#991):** the hub reads each module's `module.toml`: its pages
  show in the sidebar with the module's tree, then a **Keys** section (bound key or Set key) and
  a generated **Settings** section; buttons that open a page section or Settings go there, and
  action buttons run the module's action. A module that crashes greys its tile and page with
  "Restarting in 4 s (2 of 5)" and "Last seen 12 s ago"; a stopped one gets a red outline and
  **Open**; one that needs a newer hub says "Needs a newer Hideout" with **Update Hideout**.
  Foxglove plugins keep their settings in the hub. A file setting has **Choose file** (the
  Windows picker) beside its path field, and a module's passwords (OBS's) are typed in a masked
  field, kept in Windows Credential Manager for your user, and only ever shown as "Saved";
  **Change** then **Cancel** keeps the saved one.
- **Hideout tray flyout (#991):** a click on the Hideout tray icon opens a small panel above the
  icon (on whichever edge the taskbar is) with the module tiles, while the main window stays in
  the tray; a double click opens Hideout itself. It closes when you click elsewhere or press Esc.
  Hiding and moving tiles (··· menu, Edit tiles) is kept in `hideout.toml`. **Update Hideout**
  opens the releases page. Mic mute's tile shows a microphone icon.
  From the keyboard: the first tile has the focus when the panel opens; the arrow keys move
  between tiles, Enter opens the tile's page, Space presses its button (Focus timer's **Pause**,
  which now shows on its tile with its key) and Shift+F10 or the Menu key opens its ··· menu.
- **Update Hideout links the release a module needs (#991):** on a "Needs a newer Hideout"
  card and Modules row, **Update Hideout** opens the Hideout release that module needs when its
  catalog names one (the new optional catalog field `min_hub_release`, contract 1.11), and only
  if that link is on the Hideout releases page on github.com; otherwise it opens the releases
  page, as before.
- **Hideout: when a module crashes (#983):** its page shows an amber bar, "<Module> stopped
  unexpectedly. Restarting in 4 s (2 of 5).", counting down while the page is on screen, and goes
  away by itself once the module is back. After five crashes in ten minutes the page shows
  "Stopped after 5 crashes" with one button: **Start again**, then **Start with default
  settings**, then **Go back to <version>** (or **Turn off <Module>** when there is no older
  version), and **Copy diagnostics**. The tray icon gets a red dot only for a stopped module or
  one that needs a newer Hideout (amber in safe mode); restarts never touch it. A Windows
  notification "<Module> stopped" with **Start again** and **Open <Module>** comes once per module
  per sign-in. A module whose files fail the start check reads "Couldn't start <Module>".
  **Copy diagnostics** now copies what a bug report needs: exit codes, crash times, the steps
  already tried, the Hideout and module versions and the last 50 lines of the module's log, with
  tokens, other programs' process ids and your user folder and name left out (under 16 KB).
- **Hideout's Modules page lists your modules (#983):** each installed module with its version,
  its state in plain words ("On", "Off", "Starting…", "Restarting in 4 s (2 of 5)", "Stopped
  after 5 crashes", "Couldn't start") and **Open**.
- **Hideout: a way out of safe mode (#1029):** in safe mode the Modules page opens with the
  banner "Safe mode" / "Hideout closed 3 times in 5 minutes, so no modules were started." and
  **Start normally**, and the tray menu has **Start normally** under its status line. It turns
  safe mode off and starts your modules right away, without restarting Hideout. "Find the module
  that breaks Hideout" is shown greyed ("Comes in a later Hideout.").
- **Hideout enters safe mode by itself (#1033):** when Hideout closes without quitting (a crash,
  a kill) 3 times in 5 minutes, the next start is in safe mode: no module starts, and the
  banner says why. Quit Hideout, signing out and shutting down never count. Safe mode
  set by hand reads "Safe mode is on, so no modules were started." **Turn on** in safe mode saves
  the module as on and shows "On · not started in safe mode"; **Start normally** starts it. The
  greyed "Find the module that breaks Hideout" tells a screen reader it comes later, and Copy
  diagnostics hides your file paths whole when they have spaces in them.
- **Hideout finds the module that breaks it (#1033):** in safe mode, **Find the module that
  breaks Hideout** starts half of your modules, with a bar at the top of Hideout's pages:
  "Round 2 of 4", which modules are on, and **It's fine** · It closed · Stop finding. Use
  Hideout as usual: if it closes, the next start says "Round 2: Hideout closed." and **Start
  round 3** starts the next half on your click; after 5 minutes open (the PC awake) the round
  counts as fine and the next one starts. Quitting pauses a round (**Go on with round 2**). It
  ends with "Art seems to make Hideout close." and **Turn off Art** (or **Go back to Art
  0.4.2** when an older version is kept), with "Hideout stayed open in every round." or with
  "Hideout closed with no modules on." Your on/off list stays as it was until you choose; Stop
  finding changes nothing. The tray says which round it is and has **Start round 3** and
  **Stop finding**; with the window closed, one notification says when the result is ready.
- **Modules page buttons (#1029):** each row has one main button for its state: the stopped
  card's next step (**Start again**, then **Start with default settings**, then **Go back to
  <version>**), **Turn on**, **Update Hideout** or **Try again**, next to **Open**. Rows that need
  you come first with a red warning mark before the name, then the rest A–Z, Off last and dimmed.
- **Copy diagnostics (#1029):** adds a Windows version line, says the next step offered in words,
  and also leaves out your computer name, short user names and the names of your own files and
  folders under your user folder (outside Hideout's folder).
- **Live pages in Hideout (#976):** a module page can show a live canvas, image or text stream
  (a room map, a screen preview, an activity log). It runs only while the page is on screen, at
  most 30 frames a second (2 with Windows animation effects off, with a small "Live" dot), and
  stops when the window is minimised or another tab is shown. An out-of-date frame dims with
  "Last seen"; a stream that keeps ending shows "Stopped updating" with **Try again**.
- **Hideout pop-out windows (#998):** any page can open in its own window: **Pop out** in a
  tab's menu, the ↗ button at the tab strip's right end, Ctrl+Shift+O, or "Pop out <page>" in
  Ctrl+K. The window is titled "Hideout · <page>", groups under Hideout's taskbar button and stays
  up while Hideout is in the tray. Its tab stays in place, dimmed with ↗; a click brings the
  window forward. **Dock back** (or Ctrl+Shift+O) puts the page back as the active tab; closing
  the window puts it back quietly and never asks. **Keep on top** (the pin, off by default) and
  the window's place are remembered per page and per screen layout in `hideout.toml`, never off
  screen. Pop-outs come back after a restart; started in the tray at sign-in, they wait until you
  open Hideout.
- **Pop-out follow-ups (#1012):** a start in the tray (`--tray`) no longer flashes the main
  window. The pop-out's title row shows the page's icon before its title. Screen readers hear
  "<page> opened in its own window" and "<page> docked, tab 3 of 4" once; the focus moves with
  the page and comes back to the same element on Dock back; F6 moves between the title row and
  the page. When a module is removed (it leaves the inventory), its pop-out closes with its tab
  and the status strip says "<Module> was removed; its window was closed." for 10 seconds.
  The Modules page follows installs and removals while Hideout runs (#1054): a removed
  module's row goes at once and a newly installed one's row comes, with no restart (a new
  module still starts at the next Hideout start). A lab hub also notices a `lab-modules\`
  folder made after it started.
- **Drag pages in and out of pop-outs (#1054):** drag a tab off the strip and a card follows
  the pointer ("Let go to open in its own window" / "Esc cancels"); let go outside Hideout and
  the page opens in its own window there, its title row under the pointer. Letting go
  anywhere else in Hideout or pressing Esc cancels; on the strip the drag only reorders, as
  before. Drag a pop-out by its title row (the top of the window, or the empty part of the row
  below it) onto Hideout's tab strip: an orange bar shows where it lands, and letting go docks
  it there. Esc puts it back. The menu, button and Ctrl+Shift+O ways are unchanged.
- **Hideout tabs (#953, #1012):** each tab shows its page's icon before the title (a stopped
  page shows the red mark instead). Shift+F10 or the Menu key on a focused tab opens the same
  menu as a right-click, its first item focused; Esc goes back to the tab. **Quit Hideout** (the
  tray, or the main window's window menu) asks once, listing every page with unsaved work in the
  main window and in pop-outs: **Keep open** (the default) or Quit anyway. With nothing unsaved
  it quits at once.

## Hideout

- **Lab and Modules page fixes (#1076):** a module installed while Hideout runs now starts
  right away, as it would after a restart (also when it is turned on in a second step). The
  Modules page keeps its rows when the installed modules can't be read for a moment, and the
  log says why (`Modules page: …`). In a lab hub, **Turn off** on a lab module is saved, so it
  stays off after a restart. A double click on the tray icon opens Hideout and closes the
  flyout straight away, so it no longer flickers. A single click still opens the flyout at
  once.
- **Set key (#991):** a module page's **Keys** section binds a key to each of its actions:
  **Set key**, then press F13 to F24, or any other key with two of Ctrl, Alt and Shift
  ("Press a key… (Esc cancels)"). Everyday shortcuts such as Ctrl+C stay the apps', and a
  chord that types a character on your keyboard layout (AltGr) is refused with what it types.
  The key works in every app, also with Hideout in the tray, and its chip shows on the tile's
  button. **Change** and **Remove** sit next to a bound key; a key another action already has,
  or one another app holds, is named on the row. Leaving the row (a click elsewhere, another
  tab, another window) stops waiting. Keys are kept in `hideout.toml`.
- **Settings and privacy (#991):** a new **Settings** page (at the bottom of the sidebar, also
  in safe mode) has **Privacy**: "Hideout notices when apps that your modules work with start
  or quit, so their tiles show up. It keeps a list of those apps on this PC only."
  **Forget seen apps** clears that list (`apps-seen.txt`) at once; tiles that wait for an app
  hide until it starts again. Under **About** it shows Hideout's version.
- **OpenRGB, quiet (#991):** once OpenRGB has sent colours and stopped, its tile says
  "Last colours 3 min ago"; the age moves only while the tile or page is on screen.
- **App names (#991):** for the coming permission dialog, the hub reads a watched app's name
  from its exe's version info ("see whether OBS Studio is running" rather than "obs64"), when
  the app runs or has an App Paths entry; otherwise the exe's name without `.exe`.

- **A version its publisher withdrew (#1026):** when a module source withdraws the version you
  run, Hideout stops it and its page says "Art 0.5.0 was withdrawn", with Hideout's line, the
  publisher's own note (plain text, at most two lines) and **Check for updates**, **Go back to**
  the previous version when one is kept, and **Turn off**. The tab and the tile get the red
  mark, the tray a red dot and "Art was withdrawn", and you get one notification for that
  version, not one at every sign-in. It doesn't start again until a fixed version arrives or you
  go back; your settings and data are kept. From the keyboard, the focus goes back to **Check
  for updates** when "Checking…" ends (unless you moved it). A screen reader finds the
  withdrawn card and the stopped card as regions named by their titles (#1049).
- **The sidebar shows the orange ring too (#1094):** a module that is starting, restarting,
  updating or waiting for another one has the same ring on its sidebar entry as on its tab, with
  the same words ("Art, waiting for Link", "Art, updating…"). A stopped module's red mark still
  wins over the ring.
- **Module sources are checked (#1049):** Hideout reads each module source in its settings once
  when it starts, and the module's source again when you press **Check for updates**, never on
  a timer. A source's list is used only when its signature checks out and it isn't older than
  the last one Hideout accepted; Hideout keeps that list, so a withdrawal still applies after a
  restart without the network. With no source set, nothing is fetched and Check for updates
  says "Couldn't check", as before. Copy diagnostics names the module's source, with the list's
  number and when it was accepted.
- **Modules that wait on another, updates and quarantine (#983):** when a module another one
  needs stops (it crashed, is restarting or was turned off), the one that needs it keeps
  running and says "Waiting for <module>", with **Go to <module>**; it isn't restarted, and it
  reads normally again once the other is back. A module whose files need a newer Hideout says
  "Needs a newer Hideout" and isn't started, with **Go back to** the previous version when one
  is kept. A module whose page made Hideout close twice is stopped until you press **Start
  again** or **Go back**. An update now waits for the module: "Update ready" with **Force** and
  **Not now** while it is busy (a flash running, say), "Update waiting for you" with **Review**
  when the new version asks for more permissions (the old version keeps running, never a pop-up
  at sign-in), and "Updating…" for the moment of the swap. These states now show on the
  module's page (a card for Needs a newer Hideout and for a page that couldn't be shown, a line
  above the page for the others), its tab, its tray tile and its Modules row; the tray icon
  stays quiet for all of them but Needs a newer Hideout. A module that waits, or whose update
  waits, keeps its page live. **Review** is greyed ("Comes in a later Hideout.") until the
  permission window exists; **Not now** keeps the old version (#983, #1049).
- **A module that needs a newer Hideout is listed, not dropped (#1096):** a module whose files
  were written for a newer Hideout (with settings this one doesn't know yet) used to show on the
  Modules page by its internal id with no word of why it didn't run. It now shows by its name,
  reading "Needs a newer Hideout" with **Update Hideout**, and it isn't started.
- **Modules in a sandbox, opt-in (#983):** with `[supervisor] appcontainer = true` in
  `hideout.toml`, Hideout starts each module in a Windows AppContainer of its own when its
  permissions allow it: it reads only its own folder, writes only its own settings and data,
  and still talks to Hideout. Off by default for now; nothing changes until it is turned on.
  (#1092) With it on, the first-party plugins (OBS, Focus timer, Mic mute, OpenRGB) run
  sandboxed too, and live previews such as the Room map's work from inside the sandbox. The
  Corsair iCUE plugin runs outside it, as the permission you gave it (your files) says.
  (#1092) Modules written with the Python SDK work in the sandbox as Rust ones do: their
  channels and previews, and the same checks that they talk only to Hideout and to the
  module they were granted.
